# Threat & Error Management (TEM) — Prompts for Sim Pilots

Threat and Error Management is the framework real airline crews use to think about safety on every flight. It is not paperwork; it is a habit of anticipating what could go wrong before it does, and having a plan.

This page is a short set of prompts you can apply at each phase of a sim flight. The goal is to build the habit so that when you eventually fly real aircraft — or fly complex sim scenarios on VATSIM — it is already automatic.

---

## The three-word definition

**Threats** are conditions outside your control that increase risk (weather, terrain, unfamiliar airport, complex SID, tired pilot, sim performance issues).

**Errors** are things you or your crew do wrong (miss a callout, dial the wrong frequency, forget a checklist, mis-set an altitude).

**Undesired Aircraft States** are what happens when errors are not caught: unstable approach, altitude bust, runway excursion, controlled flight into terrain.

The goal is to identify **threats** early, catch **errors** before they become undesired states, and recover cleanly if an undesired state develops.

---

## Pre-flight prompts

Ask yourself, honestly:

- What is different about this flight from a routine flight? *(new aircraft, new airport, new sim update, first flight of the day, tired)*
- Where in the flight is my workload going to spike? *(complex SID, dense arrival, low-visibility approach, mountainous terrain)*
- What is the weather doing across the whole route? Where is my divert if the destination goes below minimums?
- Am I current on this aircraft or is this the first flight in weeks?

Name the top two threats out loud. Say what you will do about each.

## Taxi prompts

- Have I looked at the airport diagram? Do I know the hot spots?
- Am I clear on my taxi route, or am I waiting to figure it out one intersection at a time?
- Runway incursions kill more people in aviation than almost anything else. Slow down. Read every sign.

## Takeoff prompts

- Have I briefed the reject decision, out loud, in this cockpit, in the last few minutes?
- Do I know the engine-out routing for *this* runway on *this* SID?
- What am I doing with my hands when the master warning fires at 100 kt? Say it.

## Cruise prompts

Cruise is the danger zone for complacency. Use the quiet time.

- Am I ahead of the aircraft or behind it?
- Is my fuel state matching the plan? If not, why not, and what will I do about it?
- Where is the weather now vs the forecast?
- What am I going to brief for the arrival, and when?

## Descent and approach prompts

- Am I high or low on profile? What is my recovery plan?
- Have I re-briefed if anything has changed — runway, weather, approach type, aircraft configuration?
- Am I stabilised? If in doubt, go around. The go-around is a *normal manoeuvre*, not an admission of failure.

## Post-flight prompts

The most important habit. After every serious flight, ask:

- What did I do well?
- What did I do badly?
- What surprised me, and why did it surprise me?
- What will I do differently next time?

Write it down. Even three lines. You will be a better pilot in six months if you do this on every flight than if you fly ten times as many hours without ever reflecting.

# Training Scenario: Engine Failure at or After V1

**Objective:** fly a clean engine-out departure from V1 through gear-up, initial climb, and clean-up. Practice the rhythm of *aviate → navigate → communicate → run the drill*, in that order.

**Setup:** MSFS 2024 or X-Plane 12, any twin-engine transport aircraft. Runway long enough that V1 is safely achievable. ISA, no wind for the first run.

---

## Brief

Use the [takeoff briefing template](../briefings/takeoff-briefing.md). Pay special attention to the **engine-out routing** section — brief it as if you mean it, because in a moment you will.

## The rhythm

At V1 or after, when the engine fails, the sequence is always the same:

1. **Fly the aircraft.** Rudder to keep centreline / wings level. Rotate at VR normally. Do not chase pitch — set the target attitude and trim it out.
2. **Positive climb, gear up.** Same as a normal takeoff.
3. **Follow the engine-out routing** you briefed. This is why you briefed it. Do not invent one now.
4. **At acceleration altitude, level off, accelerate, clean up** to flaps up in the normal schedule.
5. **Now** — and only now — start the abnormal drill. Memory items first if applicable, then ECAM/QRH.
6. **Communicate** with ATC once the aircraft is stable and clean. Declare emergency ("MAYDAY MAYDAY MAYDAY" or "PAN PAN PAN PAN PAN PAN" as appropriate).

**The single biggest error simmers make is trying to run the QRH before the aircraft is under control and cleaned up.** Do not do this. Fly the aircraft first.

## Run 1 — engine failure at V1

1. Line up. Roll. At V1, fail an engine.
2. Continue takeoff. Rotate at VR. Target V2. Positive rate, gear up.
3. Fly the briefed engine-out routing.
4. At acceleration altitude, level off, accelerate, retract flaps on schedule.
5. Once clean, at safe altitude, start the drill.
6. Declare, request vectors, brief the approach (use the [approach briefing template](../briefings/approach-briefing.md)), fly it.

Debrief:
- Did you keep the wings level and the aircraft on runway heading, or did you drift?
- Did you rotate normally, or did you over-rotate trying to gain altitude fast?
- Did you follow the engine-out routing you briefed, or did you improvise?
- Did you try to run checklists before the aircraft was clean?

## Run 2 — engine failure just after gear-up

Same as Run 1 but fail the engine at 50-100 ft AGL, gear in transit. Slightly different problem because you have less time and you are already committed to a normal flap schedule.

## Run 3 — obstacle-limited runway

Pick an airport with terrain (LSGG, LIRF 16R, VHHH 07R). Use a shorter runway if the aircraft allows. Fail the engine at V1. Now the engine-out routing matters, because "runway heading straight ahead" may put you into terrain. This is why real crews brief the engine-out routing on every takeoff.

## Common errors

- Retracting flaps too early because you are behind the aircraft.
- Retracting flaps too late because you forget in the workload.
- Chasing V2 with pitch instead of setting the attitude and letting the aircraft settle.
- Turning before acceleration altitude "to get away from terrain" — usually makes things worse.
- Running the QRH while the aircraft is still climbing out and configured dirty.

## Debrief prompts

- Where was my attention in the first 60 seconds after the failure?
- Did I use the PM (or the monitoring half of my brain) effectively, or did I try to do everything myself?
- If the failure had been a fire instead of just a flame-out, would I have handled it the same way?

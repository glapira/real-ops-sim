# Training Scenario: Rejected Takeoff

**Objective:** internalise the low-speed vs high-speed reject decision, and practice a clean stop from high speed.

**Setup:** MSFS 2024 or X-Plane 12, any transport-category aircraft (A32NX, default 747-8, CJ4). Dry runway, ISA, no wind. Runway of at least the accelerate-stop distance for your weight.

---

## Brief

Before you touch the throttles, brief the reject decision out loud using the [takeoff briefing template](../briefings/takeoff-briefing.md).

Key point to internalise: **there are two regimes.**

- **Low-speed regime (typically below 80 kt):** reject for anything abnormal. Master caution, tyre burst, unusual noise, anything that makes you think "no". You have runway and low kinetic energy. Take it.
- **High-speed regime (above 80 kt up to V1):** reject *only* for engine failure, fire, predictive windshear, or loss of directional control. Everything else — you go. The aircraft is safer airborne than trying to stop from 130 kt on the runway you have left.

## Run 1 — low-speed reject

1. Line up, thrust set, roll.
2. At ~60 kt, trigger the reject yourself by pulling a throttle to idle. Call **"STOP"**.
3. Throttles closed → max reverse → brakes as required → maintain centreline.
4. Come to a full stop on the runway. Note the distance used.

Debrief: did you call "STOP" before you acted? The call commits both pilots (or both parts of your brain) to the decision. Acting first and calling later is how you get into a situation where one pilot is stopping and the other is still trying to fly.

## Run 2 — high-speed reject at V1 -10

1. Line up, thrust set, roll.
2. At approximately V1 -10 kt, trigger an engine failure (in the sim, fail an engine via the failure menu or pull a throttle to idle).
3. Call **"STOP"**. Throttles closed → max reverse (working engine) → brakes → rudder to maintain centreline.
4. Come to a full stop.

Debrief:
- Did you keep the aircraft on the centreline? Rudder is the primary control for directional stability during a high-speed reject on one engine.
- Did you go to max reverse on the working engine, or did you hesitate?
- How much runway did you use? Was there any left?

## Run 3 — the temptation

1. Same setup. At V1 +5 kt, trigger a minor caution (e.g. a system fault that is not engine, fire, or control).
2. **Do not reject.** Continue the takeoff. Rotate at VR. Positive rate, gear up.
3. Once safely climbing, handle the caution as an abnormal.

This is the run everyone gets wrong the first time. The instinct is to reject anything that surprises you. Above V1, the correct action is almost always to go flying and sort it out in the air.

## Common errors to watch for

- Calling "STOP" but not fully closing the throttles. The autothrottle can wake up if you leave your hand off.
- Forgetting reverse. Reverse thrust does a huge amount of the deceleration work above 80 kt.
- Coming off the centreline. On one engine at high speed, the yaw is aggressive. Use rudder early.
- Trying to reject at V1 +anything. Do not. Go.

## Debrief prompts

- Where would this reject have put me if the runway had been 500 m shorter?
- What would I have done differently if the runway were wet or contaminated?
- Did I call before I acted, or act before I called?

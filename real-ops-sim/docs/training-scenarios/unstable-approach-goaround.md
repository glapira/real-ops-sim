# Training Scenario: Unstable Approach → Go-Around

**Objective:** train the go-around as a normal manoeuvre, not as an emergency. The single biggest killer in commercial aviation approach-and-landing accidents is the continued unstable approach. The fix is simple and hard: go around when the criteria are not met, every time.

**Setup:** MSFS 2024 or X-Plane 12, any transport aircraft. Choose an ILS approach at an airport you know. Weather: pick something moderately gusty, or add a tailwind on final to make yourself high and fast.

---

## The stabilisation gate

You are stabilised by **1000 ft AAL in IMC / 500 ft AAL in VMC** if and only if:

- On the correct lateral and vertical path
- On speed (Vapp -0 / +10)
- In landing configuration (gear down, flaps set)
- Thrust set (not idle unless briefed)
- All checklists complete
- Sink rate not excessive (typically < 1000 fpm)

If any one of these is not met at the gate: **go around.** Not "try to save it". Not "give it another five seconds". Go around.

## The go-around, as a normal manoeuvre

The reason simmers (and, sadly, some real pilots) hesitate to go around is that they treat it as an admission of failure. It is not. It is a standard manoeuvre that you should practice until it is boring.

The sequence is:

1. **Call "go around, flaps [X]".** Commit verbally before physically.
2. **TOGA thrust.**
3. **Pitch to the go-around attitude** (typically ~15° nose up, or the flight director command).
4. **Positive rate, gear up.**
5. **Follow the missed approach procedure** (or ATC vectors).
6. **At acceleration altitude, level off if required, accelerate, clean up.**
7. **Configure for next approach or divert.**
8. **Communicate with ATC** — request second approach, hold, or divert.

## Run 1 — the deliberately unstable approach

1. Set up an ILS with a 15 kt tailwind on final.
2. Fly it. You will find yourself high, fast, and struggling to configure.
3. At 1000 ft AAL, honestly assess. If any stabilisation criterion is not met, **go around**.
4. Fly the missed. Reposition. Try again with wind mitigation (larger circuit, earlier configuration, speedbrake).

The point of this run is to feel what an unstable approach actually feels like at 1000 ft, so that in the future you recognise it and act on it.

## Run 2 — the low go-around

1. Fly a normal ILS.
2. At 200 ft, initiate a go-around (simulate a runway incursion, sudden windshear, or an "unable to land safely" scenario).
3. Fly the missed cleanly.

Debrief: did you push TOGA fully forward, or did you tentatively add thrust? Did the aircraft respond as you expected? What surprised you?

## Run 3 — the balked landing

1. Fly a normal approach and touchdown.
2. Just after main-gear touchdown, initiate a go-around (simulate an aircraft on the runway ahead).
3. TOGA, positive rate → gear up, fly the missed.

This is one of the highest-workload manoeuvres in the book because you are transitioning from landing configuration to climb configuration with the aircraft on the runway. Practice it in the sim so you know what to expect.

## Common errors

- Adding TOGA thrust without pitching up → aircraft accelerates on the runway or in level flight instead of climbing.
- Pitching up without adding TOGA thrust → aircraft decelerates toward stall.
- Retracting gear before positive climb → aircraft settles.
- Forgetting to change the missed approach altitude in the FCU/MCP → aircraft levels off at the wrong altitude.
- Not communicating with ATC → controller has no idea what you are doing.

## Debrief prompts

- Did I go around because the criteria said to, or did I only go around when I was already in trouble?
- What was my honest self-assessment at 1000 ft? Was I being generous with the stabilisation criteria?
- Did I fly the missed approach cleanly, or did I improvise it?
- How much did the go-around cost me in fuel and time? (Usually a lot less than most pilots think — which is the point.)
